## 0. Registration metadata

**Title.** Whose Standard of Care? Jurisdictional Defaults, Guideline Staleness and Certified Abstention of LLMs on Vietnamese Ministry of Health Guidelines

**Authors.** Binh Minh (TDTU) et al.
The remaining contributors are added on OSF before submission. They may include the supervisor and, if recruited, clinician co-authors. Authorship follows CRediT.

**Description.**
Students and clinicians in Vietnam increasingly consult large language models (LLMs). Even when explicitly asked to follow Vietnam's Ministry of Health (MoH), an LLM may answer with a value from a foreign guideline (WHO, US, European or UK) or from a superseded MoH version. We will audit open-weight and commercial LLMs against the MoH diagnosis and treatment guidance in force on the corpus freeze date (15 October 2026).

Each recommendation is broken into span-verified "atoms": a dose, threshold, duration, schedule, first-line drug, classification, target or procedure for a stated population. Each atom is linked to dated values from named foreign guideline systems, to values from superseded MoH versions, and to a decoy value generated by a fixed rule, which measures chance coincidence.

Models answer short-answer and planted-option multiple-choice questions in Vietnamese and English under context conditions A0–A6. A pre-registered rule-based grader gives every response exactly one of six labels and attributes non-concordant answers to a named foreign system or a superseded version wherever possible. No LLM is used as a judge.

The confirmatory hypotheses are:

- **H1 (primary):** under an explicit MoH cue, matches to foreign values exceed chance.
- **H2:** English prompts increase matches to US values.
- **H3:** foreign or superseded answers persist when the exact MoH passage is supplied.
- **H4:** a label-free disagreement signal separates answers that are more likely to state a non-MoH value.

A secondary aim (RQ3) reports simultaneous Clopper–Pearson upper bounds on the selective-answer risk of an abstention layer. Its guarantee holds only for questions drawn from the same frozen corpus; transfer to unseen guidelines is assessed empirically.

**Suggested tags.** large language models; clinical practice guidelines; Vietnam; jurisdictional bias; guideline versioning; selective prediction; Learn-then-Test; preregistration; TRIPOD-LLM.

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Research questions or hypotheses

**Background.**

- LLMs can default to US frameworks when a question does not name a country (Wang & Suresh, arXiv 2606.00333).
- LLMs can fail to follow non-US guidelines unless the guideline text is supplied (Bazerbachi et al., Eur Radiol 2026).
- MoH guidance differs from foreign guidance in specific, checkable values. It also changes often: in 2025–2026 alone, the hepatitis B, COPD, community-acquired pneumonia, seasonal influenza and measles guidance was replaced.

The risk is an answer that is right somewhere else, or right for an earlier version, and therefore sounds authoritative.

**Definitions used throughout.** These are fixed before any main-study data exist.

- **Reference standard.** MoH diagnosis and treatment guidance in force on the corpus freeze date (15 October 2026), for the stated population. The target is *fidelity to Vietnamese guidance*, not medical correctness.
  - Validity (effective dates, partial amendments) is recorded per atom; every atom has an effective date on or before the freeze date, checked by code at the atom freeze.
  - If two current MoH documents give different values for the same population, every current value counts as correct: the MoH value set is their union (decision rule DR8), taken only within the same level of care when a document applies to one level (for example, guidance for commune health stations).
- **Atom.** One span-verified recommendation. It records:
  - the source: guideline (number, year), section, page and verbatim span;
  - the clinical context: population, condition and intervention;
  - the slot type: dose, threshold, duration, schedule, first_line, classification, target or procedure;
  - the value kind: `num`, `bp`, `schedule`, `drugs` or `cat`;
  - the values: the MoH **value set** (a range, a set of drugs, or several co-valid values), foreign values tagged by system (US, EU_UK, WHO_global, WHO_WPRO, or OTHER for another named international body) with source and version date, superseded MoH values tagged by guideline, MoH values of **neighbouring contexts** (another step of the same protocol, another population or another level of care in the current corpus, with page and span), and one decoy;
  - the derived fields: tolerance, conflict status, conflict family, the number of foreign targets k_i, the decoy rule and the decoy checks (Sections 4.2, 6.4).
  - A value that was computed rather than read verbatim (for example, a bolus converted to a rate) is flagged `derived`. Derived values are ignored for conflict status, tolerance, k_i and attribution; a sensitivity analysis keeps them (Section 5.6 B.23).
- **Conflict atom.** `conflict_status == "conflict"`, computed only by `vnsoc.grade.conflict_status` (Section 6.2). Informally:
  - at least one foreign value lies *outside* the MoH value set; and
  - that value can be told apart from every superseded value and from the decoy.

  Atoms whose status is `concordant`, `no_counterpart` or `indistinguishable` are not conflict atoms.
- **Conflict family** (the cluster unit of H1–H3). The set of conflict atoms that share one root discrepancy, assigned by a mechanical rule (`vnsoc.match.atom_flags.family_id`): the same slot type, value kind and unit, the same MoH value set and the same nearest conflicting foreign value. For example, every atom whose MoH clinic blood-pressure threshold of 140/90 mmHg conflicts with the US value of 130/80 mmHg forms one family, whatever its guideline. Every conflict atom has a family; the atom freeze is refused otherwise. Families can span guidelines; the family × guideline cross-tabulation and the number of families spanning more than one guideline are reported in the freeze addendum.
- **Decoy.** A value of the same kind and unit as the MoH value, about as far from it as the nearest conflicting foreign value, that belongs to no recorded source (including MoH values of neighbouring contexts). It is generated by a fixed rule (Section 6.4) and measures how often an answer hits a non-MoH value by chance. Conflict atoms for which the rule yields no acceptable decoy are excluded from H1 and H2 and counted in the freeze addendum.
- **Response labels.** Each response receives exactly one label. The labels are checked in the fixed priority order below (protocol §1.2; `vnsoc.grade`, grader_version 1.0.0 as frozen):

  | Label | Name | Meaning |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1 | correct and context-aware | Gives the MoH value and explicitly attributes a different value to a named foreign body or country, or asks which country applies (the latter only at A0) |
  | 2 | correct | Contained in the current MoH value set |
  | 3 | version drift | Matches a superseded MoH version |
  | 4 | foreign match | Matches at least one named foreign system; the matching system(s) and source version(s) are recorded |
  | 5 | unattributed | A value matching no recorded source; a match to the decoy lands here and is flagged `decoy_match` |
  | 6 | abstain | No value given |

- **Terminology.** Labels 3–5 are called *non-concordant with MoH guidance*, never "incorrect", "wrong" or "harmful". Label 6 is a *model self-abstention*.
- **Conditions.**

  | Condition | Context given to the model | Role |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | A0 | No context and no country cue | Descriptive "default standard" |
  | A1 | No context, with the cue "According to the current diagnosis and treatment guidelines of the Vietnamese Ministry of Health" | Confirmatory for H1 and H2 |
  | A2 | Retrieval-augmented generation over the MoH corpus, with a required citation | The deployment answer served for RQ3 and H4 |
  | A3 | The exact MoH passage containing the answer | H3 |
  | A4 | The full relevant chapter | Comparison with Bazerbachi et al. |
  | A5 | Retrieval over a mixed MoH + WHO/US corpus | Exploratory |
  | A6 | A vignette set in a Vietnamese hospital, with no MoH cue | Exploratory, RQ4 |

  A1 is a controlled probe, not the way students usually ask; A0 and A6 are closer to practice and are descriptive or exploratory here.

**Research questions.**

- **RQ1. Measurement and attribution.** When asked explicitly to follow the MoH, how often do LLMs answer conflict atoms with a foreign value or a superseded MoH value?
- **RQ2. Mechanism.** With context (retrieval, the exact passage, the full chapter), how much deviation remains, and does it come from retrieval failure or from the model keeping its own value?
- **RQ3. Control (secondary).** Does a label-free conflict signal separate high-risk answers, and what selective-answer risk can be certified at fixed coverage levels?
- **RQ4. Medical education (descriptive).** How are deviations distributed over the national framework of 128 core clinical problems? Do vignettes set in a Vietnamese hospital (with no "according to MoH") behave like A1?

**Hypotheses.** H1 is the single primary confirmatory hypothesis. H2, H3 and H4 form the secondary confirmatory family, corrected with Holm's step-down procedure (Holm 1979). Everything else is descriptive or exploratory.

H1 and H2 are confirmed at **A1**, not A0. At A0 the question names no country, so a US-value answer is not necessarily wrong. The table uses these symbols:

- *Open models* are the open-weight models actually run: four are planned (Section 6.7).
- π_f is the foreign-match rate and π_d is the chance-coincidence (decoy) rate. Both are defined in Section 4.3.
- **One-sided 0.025** means a one-sided test at level 0.025, equivalent to the two-sided 95% confidence-interval convention of the protocol.

| | Hypothesis | Analysis set | Outcome | Decision criterion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | Under A1, Vietnamese questions, pooled open models: answers match foreign values more often than they match the chance-coincidence control | Conflict atoms with a frozen decoy; one A1 Vietnamese short-answer response per atom and model | Δ = π_f − π_d | Supported iff the registered intersection–union rule holds (Section 5.1.1): the lower limits of the two-sided 95% family-clustered BCa, expanded-BCa and cluster-jackknife-t intervals of Δ are all > 0, **and** the wild cluster bootstrap-t one-sided p ≤ 0.025 |
| **H2** (value-level replication of Wang & Suresh) | Under A1, English questions yield more US-value matches than Vietnamese questions | US-unique conflict atoms with a frozen decoy (Section 4.3); pairs (atom × open model) with valid English and Vietnamese responses | P(label 4 with system US): English minus Vietnamese | Clustered McNemar (Durkalski) one-sided p-value, or the exact sign-flip test when fewer than 20 families have a discordant pair; confirmed iff its Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.025 |
| **H3** | Under A3 (exact MoH passage supplied), Vietnamese questions: the rate of foreign or superseded answers stays above 5% in at least half of the open models | Conflict atoms; one A3 Vietnamese short-answer response per atom and model | Per model, r_m = P(label 3 or 4) | Per-model one-sided tests of r_m ≤ 0.05 using a clustered exact (design-effect Clopper–Pearson) interval, combined by a partial-conjunction p-value for "at least ⌈M/2⌉ of M models" (Section 5.1.4); confirmed iff its Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.025 |
| **H4** | With every question answered, the disagreement group's risk of stating a non-MoH value is at least twice the agreement group's | Served A2 answers that state a value (labels 1–5; question language, Vietnamese and English) of the open models; all frozen atoms except `indistinguishable` ones | RR_MH = Mantel–Haenszel risk ratio (strata: model × language) of a non-MoH value (labels 3–5) | One-sided test of log RR_MH > log 2 using a bootstrap clustered by guideline and the registered interval rule; confirmed iff its Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.025 (the protocol's "cluster CI lower bound > 2") |

**Pre-registered descriptive aims (not confirmatory).**

- A0 "default standard" by language.
- Version drift as a continuous function of the months between guideline issue and model training cutoff.
- A "latest-version" bias check.
- The context ladder A0 → A4 and the split of A2 errors into retrieval errors versus in-context errors.
- RQ3's certified bounds at fixed coverage (the main RQ3 output), Learn-then-Test thresholds, and comparators.
- RQ4.

These are specified in Section 5.6.

**What will not be claimed.**

- That any guideline is medically better. The desired behaviour is the context-aware answer (label 1), which is measured directly.
- That a foreign-value match proves the model learned the value from that source. Attribution is value-level coincidence beyond chance, not provenance.
- Any prevalence beyond the frozen atom set. The corpus and the atoms are a purposive, conflict-enriched sample of current MoH recommendations (Section 3.1), so π_f describes that sample, not MoH guidance at large.
- That an H1 "support" is informative by itself. With a pilot-scale effect it is nearly certain; the substantive quantities are π_f and the excess over chance E with their intervals, read against a pre-specified smallest effect of interest (Section 5.3).
- Anything about the content of Vietnam's national licensing examination.
- That the method transfers to other countries.
- A theoretical guarantee for guidelines not seen during calibration, or for questions outside the frozen corpus.

## 2. Research design

### 2.1 Study type

**Selected:** "Non-randomized study" and "Other".

**Other:** an observational audit of LLM outputs against a document corpus. The items are guideline recommendations, not people.

- Every item receives every level of the within-item manipulations: model × language × context condition × question format, fully crossed for the open-weight models.
- No random assignment is needed or used.
- Each response is scored against the corpus by fixed rules.

### 2.2 Intention for causal interpretation

**Selected:** "Indirect inference on causal relationship(s)".

- The language and condition contrasts (H2, H3, the context ladder) are within-item interventions on the prompt. For the tested model versions and question set they identify the effect of the prompt change on the answer.
- Attributing an answer to a named foreign system or superseded version (H1) is value-level coincidence beyond chance. It does not show *why* a model produced the value, nor that it learned it from that source. This is stated as a limitation.

### 2.3 Blinding of experimental treatments

**Selected:** "Researchers or observers who code or interpret data for analysis will not be aware of the assigned treatments during coding."

**How coding is kept blind.**

- Coding is done by code: a deterministic, pre-registered rule-based grader (`vnsoc.grade`, grader_version 1.0.0). It is frozen before any main-study output is seen and is applied identically to every response.
- The grader uses the condition in only one place, as registered: asking "which country?" counts as label 1 only at A0.
- Three human coding tasks exist:
  - the manual check of the answer extractor (500 responses, human gate HG6.2);
  - the manual check of label-1 precision (200 responses, Section 3.1 item 7);
  - the second-pass check of conflict atoms (HG3.13).
- For the two response checks, the checker sees the question, the atom's reference values, the output and the parsed value. The model identity and the condition label are hidden, the retrieval citation line (`NGUỒN:`/`SOURCE:`), which would reveal A2, is removed, and items are shown in a seeded random order (`vnsoc.analysis.extractor_check`). The answer language remains visible, because it is the language of the question.
- The conflict-atom checks use atoms, not model outputs, and are finished before any main-study output exists.

### 2.4 Additional blinding during research or analysis

1. **Freeze before data.**
   - The corpus is frozen on 15 October 2026.
   - Atoms v1, questions v1, grading rules (`configs/grading.yaml`, grader_version 1.0.0) and prompts/conditions (`configs/conditions.yaml`) are frozen with SHA-256 checksums (`data/frozen/SHA256SUMS`) *before* any main-study model run.
2. **Outcome-blind construction.** Decoys follow a fixed rule for numeric and blood-pressure atoms, or are checked by code for the other value kinds, before any output exists. Tolerance, conflict status, conflict families, k_i, decoy checks and the A3 passage flags are computed by code. The manual context check of 100% of conflict atoms, the decoy-plausibility rating, the neighbouring-value records and clinician review (if available) finish before the question freeze.
3. **RQ3 signals use no labels.** Groups use only model outputs and retrieved passages, never grades. Thresholds and the score model use only the reference split, which is never used for certification.
4. **Tested analysis code.** The confirmatory analysis code runs on simulated data before real data, and its hash is quoted in Section 5.0.
5. **Deviations are documented.** Any deviation from this plan is recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, filed as an OSF addendum (`prereg/addenda/`) and reported in the paper. Unplanned analyses are labelled exploratory.

### 2.5 Study design

**Items.** Items are atoms drawn from 25–35 current MoH guidelines, selected by the mechanical order of Section 3.1. Superseded versions supply version values only and are not counted in the 25–35.

- Target: at least 400 conflict atoms in at least 25 conflict families (DR2 applies otherwise).
- Expected total: 2,000–3,500 atoms, all statuses.
- Atoms are nested in guidelines. Conflict atoms are also nested in conflict families, which can span guidelines.

**Questions per atom.**

- A short-answer question (the main format) in Vietnamese and English. The last line must be `ĐÁP ÁN: <giá trị> <đơn vị>` in Vietnamese, or `ANSWER: <value> <unit>` in English.
- For atoms that are conflict atoms or have a superseded value: a planted-option multiple-choice question in two option orders, in both languages (Section 6.5).
- For RQ4 (exploratory): a vignette set in a Vietnamese district hospital.

**Factors, all within item.**

- Model: 4 open-weight models, plus 1–2 low-cost API models and 1–2 frontier API models.
- Language: Vietnamese, English.
- Condition: A0–A6.
- Format: short answer, multiple choice (2 orders), vignette.
- Sample index at A2: one greedy answer plus 5 sampled answers.

**Run matrix** (protocol §5.2; cut order under DR6 in Section 5.1.9):

| Models | Atoms | Conditions × languages | Formats |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 open models | All atoms | A0–A3 × VI/EN | Short answer; multiple choice at A1 (2 orders) for conflict and superseded-value atoms; 5 sampled A2 answers for the RQ3 signal; A4 on a 300-question subset for Qwen3 and Llama |
| Low-cost API (1–2 models) | Stratified subset of 1,000–1,500 atoms: all conflict atoms plus random controls | A0–A3 × VI/EN | Short answer |
| Frontier API (1–2 models) | Conflict atoms | A0/A1 × VI/EN | Short answer |
| Exploratory (if time allows) | — | A5, A6 | — |

**Decoding.**

- Greedy (temperature 0.0), maximum 128 new tokens, seed 0.
- Qwen3's thinking mode is disabled (`enable_thinking=False`).
- API models use their minimal reasoning setting.
- Token counts and the finish reason are logged for every call.
- The 5 RQ3 signal samples at A2 use temperature 0.7 and top_p 0.95.

**Counterbalancing.** Each multiple-choice question is asked in two option orders: a seeded permutation, and its exact reverse, so every option changes letter.

**Analysis sets.**

### 2.6 Randomization

There is no random assignment: every condition is applied to every item. The only random procedures are seeded and are fixed here.

- **Multiple-choice option order** (`vnsoc.qgen.mcq.orders`).
  - Order 0 permutes the four options with `random.Random(h).shuffle`, where h is the integer value of the first 12 hexadecimal digits of SHA-256(`"20260926|<atom_id>"`). The seed 20260926 is `qgen.mcq_order_seed`.
  - Order 1 is the exact reverse of order 0.
- **RQ3 splits** (`confirmatory.assign_rq3_split`, `confirmatory.split_map`).
  - Unit: the atom. Every language and format of an atom falls on the same side.
  - Fractions: reference 0.2, calibration 0.4, test 0.4 (`rq3.split_fractions`, fixed by this registration). Seed: 20261001 (`rq3.split_seed`).
  - Procedure: (1) `split_by_atom(U, frac_cal=0.2, seed=20261001)` on the set U of all frozen atoms that have a short-answer question gives the reference split; (2) `split_by_atom(U minus reference, frac_cal=0.5, seed=20261002)` gives the calibration split; the rest is the test split.
  - The labels are written into the frozen question file (`Question.split`) at the question freeze, before any model run.
  - **Cluster-split sensitivity:** the same procedure applied to clusters (conflict family, otherwise guideline) instead of atoms; every atom takes its cluster's side.
- **Regime (a) re-randomisations.** Re-draw i = 0, …, 499 splits the non-reference atoms into calibration and test halves with `split_by_atom(non-reference atoms, 0.5, seed=[20261001, 1, i])`.
- **Leave-guideline-out splits (RQ3 regime b).** Split i, for i = 0, …, 499, permutes the sorted unique guideline identifiers with `numpy.random.default_rng([20261001, i])`. The first max(1, round(0.2·N_g)) guidelines form the reference split, the next max(1, round(0.4·N_g)) the calibration split, and the rest the test split, where N_g is the number of guidelines.
- **Cluster bootstrap and wild bootstrap.** Resampling uses multinomial cluster weights drawn from `numpy.random.default_rng(20261001)`; the wild cluster bootstrap draws Rademacher signs from the same generator, or enumerates all 2^C sign vectors when 2^C ≤ B (**Operationalisation:** the project seed is reused). The sign-flip test of Section 5.1.3 enumerates every sign vector and needs no random numbers. GroupKFold (Section 5.1.7) is deterministic.
- **Blinded check sheets.** Items are shuffled with `random.Random(20261001)`.
- **Model sampling.** Sampling seeds for the A2 signal samples are fixed per request and stored in the run records.
- **Known nondeterminism.** Batched GPU inference may be slightly nondeterministic even with greedy decoding. Each request is run once, and the recorded output is the observation; there are no reruns except after errors (Section 5.5).

## 3. Sampling

### 3.1 Data collection procedures

**1. Guideline corpus** (protocol §3.1–3.2).

- **Catalogue.** All candidate MoH decisions and circulars (75 catalogued, 52 current) are classified: official PDF or not, text layer or scanned, or only visible on unofficial aggregators (`results/tables/corpus_triage.csv`, hashed in Section 5.0).
- **Sources.** Documents are downloaded only from official sources (kcb.vn, moh.gov.vn, vncdc.gov.vn, or official provincial health department and hospital pages), with the URL, download date and SHA-256 recorded.
- **Selection (Operationalisation of protocol §3.1 items 2–3; `vnsoc.extract.corpus_priority`).** Eligible documents are current, have an official PDF, are not only on a private site, and were issued on or before 15 October 2026. They are ranked, ties broken by document key:
  1. documents cited by a seed-table row (not removed) or by a pilot atom — the operational meaning of "conflict-rich topic";
  2. text-layer PDFs issued in 2025–2026;
  3. other text-layer PDFs, most recent first;
  4. scanned PDFs, most recent first.

  Documents are taken in this order until 35 are included, with at most 10 OCR documents in total (current or superseded). DR2 additions, if needed, are the next eligible documents in the same order. Superseded versions are added for the version analysis.
- **OCR.** Every OCR value is checked by hand against the page image.
- **Supersession chains** are built from each document's own "replaces" clauses and recorded per atom, including partial amendments. The chains are checked a second time independently.
- **Freeze.** The corpus is frozen on **15 October 2026**. Documents issued later are not added (DR7).
- **Unofficial aggregators** are never scraped.

**2. Atoms** (protocol §3.4, §4.1).

- **Extraction.** A low-cost LLM extracts atoms at temperature 0 with a fixed JSON schema, one section at a time.
- **Automatic span check.** An atom is kept only if its value appears verbatim in the source span after number normalisation (`span_verified`).
- **Context check.** The student checks 100% of conflict atoms and their foreign values against a written rubric, confirming that population, disease and intervention match (`context_checked`). The rubric also requires:
  - conditional clauses in the MoH text ("có thể thấp hơn nếu dung nạp được" (may be lower if tolerated), "tối đa" (at most), "cân nhắc" (consider)) to be encoded in the value set as an open bound, or the atom to be removed;
  - MoH values of neighbouring contexts in the current corpus (another step, population or level of care) to be recorded with page and span (`moh_neighbour`);
  - values that are computed rather than read verbatim to be flagged `derived`;
  - the categorical population attributes that decide the MoH value (for example HBeAg status, compensated versus severe shock, clinic versus home measurement, trimester, G6PD status, level of care) to be listed with Vietnamese and English synonyms (`required_terms`);
  - for drug and category atoms, whether the MoH text lists a preferred option, acceptable options or an exhaustive list (`moh_scope`);
  - the atom's acuity from the registered topic list (high: shock, anaphylaxis, severe malaria, sepsis; otherwise not high).
- **Second pass.** A random 20% of conflict atoms is checked again at least one week later, giving an agreement estimate (Cohen's κ).
- **Stratified quality check.** A 200-atom sample (100 random, 100 conflict) is checked. Precision is reported with a Clopper–Pearson interval. A lower bound below 0.90 triggers DR1. The number of false conflicts among the 100 conflict atoms checked feeds the tipping-point analysis (Section 5.6 B.20).
- **Freeze checks.** Before the atom freeze, `python -m vnsoc.match.atom_flags check` must report no problem: every atom has an effective date on or before 15 October 2026; tolerance and conflict status equal their recomputation by code; every conflict atom has passed the context check, carries its mechanical family, and, if it has a decoy, its decoy rule, roundness flag and plausibility rating; drug and category conflict atoms carry `moh_scope`. The freeze addendum reports counts per decoy rule, decoy-less conflict atoms, derived values, neighbour overlaps, decoy plausibility and the family × guideline cross-tabulation.

**3. Counterparts, decoys and status** (protocol §3.3, §4.2).

- **Foreign value store.** It is versioned. Each record holds the system, source, version date, URL, location in the source, fetch date and page SHA-256. Only values and citations are stored, never passage text.
  - WHO systems: current and previous WHO versions, and WHO Western Pacific Region.
  - US and EU/UK sources.
  - Other named bodies where relevant (for example RCUK and WAO/EAACI for anaphylaxis).
- **Superseded MoH values** come from the superseded documents.
- **Decoys** are generated by rule and checked by code (Section 6.4). The rule used is frozen with the atom.
- **Decoy plausibility (HG3.5).** For 100% of conflict atoms with a decoy, the student rates the decoy against a written rubric, blind to any output: same granularity as the source values; inside the range of doses or thresholds ever used for the parameter; not equal to a neighbouring MoH value or to a known foreign or historical value. An implausible decoy is regenerated by the pre-specified rule of Section 6.4 (round to the source granularity, else borrow the foreign value of another atom of the same kind) and rated again. The rating and its reason are frozen with the atom.
- **A3 passages.** Code flags every oracle passage that itself states a foreign, superseded, decoy or neighbouring MoH value (`vnsoc.qgen.qc.passage_alt_values`; QC-table column `passage_has_alt_value`). Flagged passages are re-checked by hand before the freeze; H3 is reported with and without them (Section 5.6 B.22).
- **Tolerance and conflict status** are computed by `vnsoc.match.decoys.finalize`, which calls `vnsoc.grade`.
- **Matching quality.** Sensitivity is measured on the seed table. Precision is measured on 100 randomly chosen automatic pairs, checked by hand, with a Clopper–Pearson interval.

**4. Clinician review (if available, human gate HG3.9).** A physician reviews all conflict atoms before the question freeze and assigns:

- `clinician_confirmed`: the atom and its foreign values are clinically correct as recorded (yes/no);
- `moh_lags_evidence`: the conflicting foreign value reflects evidence newer than the MoH value (yes/no/uncertain);
- `clinical_harm`: acuity (low, moderate, high) × direction (the foreign value is more or less aggressive than the MoH value).

If no clinician is recruited, the study proceeds under DR12 with code surrogates (Section 4.2): `moh_older_than_counterpart` in place of `moh_lags_evidence`, the code-computed direction and the student-assigned acuity. These are labelled surrogates, not clinical judgements. An AI agent role-playing a clinician is never reported as clinician review.

**5. Questions** (protocol §3.5, §4.3).

- **Writing.** Questions are built from fixed templates per slot type and then paraphrased by an LLM. Numbers, units and population are preserved, which code checks. For drug and category atoms the template follows `moh_scope` (for example, "the preferred regimen" when the MoH text states a preference).
- **Code quality checks.** Questions are removed if they:
  - leak the answer (state the MoH, foreign, superseded or decoy value);
  - omit a number of the population description, or omit a required categorical population attribute (`required_terms`, in each language);
  - differ between Vietnamese and English in numbers or negation, checked by machine back-translation.
- **Ambiguity rule.** A question whose stated population would make the foreign value also correct under MoH guidance is rewritten or removed. The level of care is a required population attribute when the document applies to one level. The student checks 100% of the questions of conflict atoms for ambiguity and records the result (`Question.ambiguity_check`).
- **Other outputs.**
  - An oracle passage of 150–300 words around the span, for A3, with its passage flags.
  - Retrieval chunks of at most 500 tokens with the gold chunk identified, for A2.
  - At least 100 vignettes checked by hand, for A6.
  - Split labels.
  - A question-QC table, including English questions edited by hand after machine translation (`Question.translation_hand_edited`).
- **Freeze.** Questions, grading rules and conditions are frozen together (human gate HG4.2, then task T4.3). The freeze addendum is filed on OSF.

**6. Model runs** (planned weeks 7–9: 12 November – 2 December 2026).

- **Open models** run with vLLM on Kaggle (2 × T4 GPUs, one process per GPU), in the registered run order qwen3_8b → sailor2_8b → vistral_7b → llama31_8b.
  - Model weights and the vLLM version are pinned.
  - If vLLM cannot run on T4, HF transformers with 4-bit weights is used (DR10).
- **API models** run through batch endpoints under a hard budget cap of 40 USD.
- **Record per call:** model identifier and version, date, prompt hash, retrieved chunk IDs, raw output, tokens in/out, finish reason, and the mean token log-probability of the answer span (open models).
- **Completion check.** A run is complete when at least 98% of planned requests per model × condition × language × format cell are valid and error-free (`vnsoc.check runs`).

**7. Grading** (planned weeks 9–10).

- All responses are graded with the frozen grader. No main-study output is graded before the run matrix is complete or the DR6 cuts are fixed.
- Responses flagged `needs_llm` go to a fixed LLM answer-extraction prompt (Section 6.3). The prompt contains the question and the output only, never a reference value.
- A sample of 500 responses, stratified by language, model and condition, is checked by hand to validate the extractor (HG6.2, blinded as in Section 2.3). DR9 is evaluated overall and separately per language: it applies if extraction errors exceed 5%, or `needs_llm` exceeds 5% for any model or any language.
- **Label-1 precision.** 200 responses graded label 1, stratified by language (VI/EN) × condition (A0/A1), 50 per stratum or all if fewer, are checked by hand, blinded as in Section 2.3. The precision of label 1 is reported per stratum with a Clopper–Pearson interval.

### 3.2 Sample size

Sizes are given per level. Actual counts are fixed by the atom freeze and reported in the freeze addendum.

**Documents.** 25–35 current MoH guidelines, plus their superseded versions. At most 10 documents use OCR.

**Atoms.**

- Expected: 2,000–3,500.

- Cluster sizes are unequal (a family may hold 1 to dozens of atoms).
- Pilot atoms (about 60–70) are included and flagged.

- 2 short-answer questions (VI, EN).

- Multiple-choice responses and the A4 subset (300 questions, 2 models) come on top.

**Responses from API models.**

- Low-cost models: 1,000–1,500 atoms × 2 languages × 4 conditions.

**Analysis units per hypothesis.**

- **H1.** One A1 Vietnamese response per conflict atom with a decoy per open model. For example, 400 × 4 = 1,600 responses in at least 25 clusters.
- **H2.** Pairs (atom × open model) on US-unique conflict atoms with a decoy. Their number is unknown before matching and may be small; low power is acknowledged in advance.
- **H3.** One A3 Vietnamese response per conflict atom per open model.

- **RQ3.** Per open model, the calibration split holds 40% of atoms and the test split 40%.

### 3.3 Sample size rationale

The corpus size is bounded by the number of current MoH guidelines with official PDFs, plus the resource limits: 50–80 Kaggle GPU hours, a hard cap of 40 USD on paid APIs, and one student investigator.

**Conflict-atom target (protocol §3.6).** The primary outcome is an *unconditional* rate: the denominator is conflict atoms, not errors.

- Unequal clusters imply a design effect of 2–6.
- With 400 conflict atoms and a design effect of 3, the 95% CI half-width for a single proportion near 0.3–0.5 is about ±8 percentage points: 1.96·√(p(1−p)·3/400) = 0.078 at p = 0.3 and 0.085 at p = 0.5.

**Simulated operating characteristics (the registered estimators at the registered B = 10,000).** `prereg/analysis_plan/simulate_operating_characteristics.py` runs the confirmatory code on data from `simulate_study` and writes `prereg/analysis_plan/operating_characteristics.json` (never edited by hand; table in `prereg/analysis_plan/README.md` §6). The null scenarios keep self-abstentions concentrated in the disagreement group, model-specific disagreement shares and base risks, k_i = 2 targets on 30% of conflict atoms and 5% decoy-less conflict atoms. Designs: 40 × 10 and 25 × 16 families × atoms, 25 families with lognormal sizes, and 12 × 10 (fewer than 20 families). The registered check is that every rejection rate under a null is at most 0.025 + 2·MCSE.

### 3.4 Starting and stopping rules

**End of the pilot.** The pilot ends with the FMC abstract (30 September 2026). Its outputs are never used as main-study data.

**Start of main data collection.** Main collection starts only when all of these hold:

1. this registration is submitted;
2. the corpus is frozen (15 October 2026);
3. atoms v1, questions v1, grading rules and conditions are frozen with checksums, and the freeze addendum is filed;
4. the pre-run review (milestone M2) is passed.

**Stopping.** There is no optional stopping and no interim look at outcomes.

- The full planned run matrix is executed once. No main-study output is graded before the matrix is complete or the DR6 cuts are fixed.
- Collection ends when every planned cell has at least 98% valid records, or when the rule for incomplete cells applies (Section 5.4).
- The only scope reductions are resource-driven and outcome-blind, following DR6:
  - **trigger:** forecast GPU use > 80 hours, spending > 38 USD, or a delay > 1 week;
  - **cut order:** A5/A6 → A4 → frontier API models reduced to 1 → low-cost API models reduced to 1 → open models reduced to 3;
  - **which open model is dropped (Operationalisation):** the last model in the registered run order that has not completed its A1 and A3 runs, that is llama31_8b unless it has already been run;
  - RQ1 with A1 and A3 is always kept.
- Model replacements follow DR10 and DR11 (Section 6.7).
- If fewer than 400 conflict atoms or 25 families exist after the freeze, collection proceeds with what exists (DR2), and the definition of conflict is never loosened.

- `core_problem_id` (RQ4, if mapped).

| U_r | 1{label = 5} | Unattributed |

| D_r | 1{decoy_match} | Decoy match (**Operationalisation:** any response with a parsed value matching the decoy, whatever its label) |
| FUS_r | 1{label = 4 and "US" ∈ foreign_systems} | US-value match |

The losses are computed from the label inside the analysis code, never supplied upstream.

**Number of foreign targets, k_i.** For conflict atom i, k_i ≥ 1 is the number of distinct conflicting foreign value items.

- An item is *conflicting* if it lies outside the MoH set: its gap to every MoH item is > 0. Derived items are not counted.
- Items identical across systems (gap 0) are merged, exactly as in `vnsoc.qgen.mcq._conflicting` (`vnsoc.match.atom_flags.k_foreign`).

**H1 rates and excess over chance.**

- The foreign-match rate is π_f = Σ_{m,i} F_mi / N.
- The chance-coincidence rate is π_d = Σ_{m,i} k_i·D_mi / N.
- Here N is the number of valid responses on conflict atoms with a decoy, pooled over the open models (**Operationalisation:** response-level pooling).
- Δ = π_f − π_d.
- Excess over chance: E = (π_f − π_d) / (1 − π_d) (protocol §1.2).

**Operationalisation: why D is multiplied by k_i.** Label 4 is met by matching *any* of the k_i foreign targets, while each atom has only one decoy. Under pure chance with equally likely targets, E[F] = k_i·E[D]. The decoy mirrors the *nearest* conflicting foreign value, and farther targets are, if anything, less likely to be hit by chance. So the scaling is conservative. When every k_i = 1 the definition reduces to the protocol's literal one, which is reported as a sensitivity analysis (Section 5.6 B.7).

**Multiple-choice analogue.**

- F^MCQ is choosing an option whose role is foreign.
- D^MCQ is choosing the decoy option.
- k^MCQ_i is the number of foreign-role options (1 or 2).
- Each atom's two orders are averaged, and Δ_MCQ is formed as above.

**US-unique atom (H2) — Operationalisation** (`vnsoc.match.atom_flags.us_unique`). A conflict atom that:

- has at least one conflicting US value item; and
- where every conflicting US item is distinguishable from every non-US foreign item, every superseded item and the decoy: gap ≥ 2·tolerance for `num`/`bp`, gap > 0 for the other kinds, with `_gap` as defined in `vnsoc.grade`.

On such atoms, FUS can only mean the US value.

**Stubbornness rate** (protocol §4.4). S = P(label ∈ {3, 4} | A3), over atoms that have a distinct foreign or superseded value.

- H3 uses conflict atoms, Vietnamese.
- The descriptive index also covers version-drift atoms, meaning non-conflict atoms with a distinguishable superseded value.

**Retrieval error at A2** (protocol §4.4).

| Quantity | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Retrieval error | 1{gold chunk not in the top 5} · W |
| In-context error | 1{gold chunk in the top 5} · W |
| Retrieval recall@5 | P(gold chunk in the top 5) |

**RQ3.**

- Disagreement group: G_i = 1 if s1 ∨ s2 ∨ s3, else agreement (G = 2 groups, fixed in advance).
- **Signal equality — Operationalisation.** Parsed values are compared after unit normalisation:
  - "differs" means the normalised value sets are not identical. No tolerance is used, because tolerance depends on reference values.
  - A value and a no-value answer count as different; two no-value answers count as equal.
  - s3 fires when the answer states a value and either no cited passage contains an identical parsed value or no valid citation is given. It does not fire when no value is stated.
  - Because served answers that state no value are outside the primary H4 and RQ3 populations, a self-abstention can never be counted both as "disagreement" and as an error.
- Consistency: the fraction of the 5 samples whose parsed value equals the greedy answer's.
- Score c(x): see Section 5.1.7.
- Selective risk: R_g(λ) = P(E = 1 | V = 1, group g, c(x) ≥ λ).
- Coverage: P(c(x) ≥ λ | V = 1, group g). The model's self-abstention rate P(V = 0) is reported next to it, so overall coverage = (1 − self-abstention) × layer coverage.

**H4 risk ratio.** RR_MH = Σ_s (a_s·n0_s/N_s) / Σ_s (c_s·n1_s/N_s), where s runs over the strata model × language, a_s and c_s are the numbers of value-stating served answers with E = 1 in the disagreement and agreement groups, n1_s and n0_s the group sizes and N_s = n1_s + n0_s.

**Months to cutoff (exploratory).** The number of months from the issue date of the current MoH guideline to the model's documented training-data cutoff. It is negative when the guideline was issued after the cutoff and is treated as continuous.

**Latest-version bias (exploratory).** For conflict atoms with at least two dated versions of the same foreign system: P(match to the latest version) − P(match to an older version), from `foreign_sources`.

**Harm-weighted foreign rate (only if a clinician assigns `clinical_harm`).** Σ w_i·F_i / Σ w_i, with acuity weights low = 1, moderate = 2, high = 3 (**Operationalisation**). The direction (more versus less aggressive than MoH) is reported separately.

**Rates also reported.** Awareness rate P(label 1) with its checked precision, self-abstention rate P(label 6), answer-line compliance P(parse_method = answer_line), truncation rate, and the rate of answers equal to the exact recorded MoH value (Section 5.6 B.18).

### 5.1 Statistical models

#### 5.1.1 Common inferential machinery

**Cluster bootstrap** (Field & Welsh 2007).

- Resample whole clusters with replacement: conflict families for H1 and H2; guidelines for H4. Each resampled cluster brings all its atoms and all their model responses. This is implemented as multinomial cluster weights on per-cluster sums (`confirmatory.cluster_interval`).
- Number of replicates: B = 10,000 (**Operationalisation**; chosen so that Monte Carlo error is small at the Holm level 0.025/3). Seed as in Section 2.6.

**BCa interval** (Efron 1987).

- Bias correction: z0 = Φ⁻¹( (#{θ*_b < θ̂} + 0.5·#{θ*_b = θ̂}) / B ).
- Acceleration: a = Σ_j (θ̄ − θ_(j))³ / (6·[Σ_j (θ̄ − θ_(j))²]^{3/2}), where θ_(j) is the estimate with cluster j deleted and θ̄ is their mean.
- The interval takes the Ĝ⁻¹ quantiles at Φ(z0 + (z0 + z_α)/(1 − a(z0 + z_α))), where Ĝ is the bootstrap distribution function.
- One-sided p by BCa inversion (`confirmatory._bca_pvalue`): with Ĝ(θ0) = (#{θ*_b < θ0} + 0.5·#{θ*_b = θ0}) / B clipped to [0.5/B, 1 − 0.5/B] and w = Φ⁻¹(Ĝ(θ0)) − z0, p = Φ(w/(1 + a·w) − z0), or p = Ĝ(θ0) if 1 + a·w ≤ 0.

**Expanded BCa.** The same resamples, with the normal quantiles replaced by √(C/(C − 1))·t_{C−1} quantiles, and the p-value inverted accordingly.

**Cluster jackknife-t.** SE_J = √((C − 1)/C · Σ_j (θ_(j) − θ̄)²); interval θ̂ ± t_{0.975, C−1}·SE_J; one-sided p = 1 − F_{t, C−1}((θ̂ − θ0)/SE_J).

**Wild cluster restricted bootstrap-t** (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2008; MacKinnon & Webb 2017), for H1, whose estimator is a ratio of cluster sums, θ = Σ_c z_c / Σ_c n_c with z_c = Σ_{i∈c}(F_i − k_i·D_i):

- t = (θ̂ − θ0)/SE with SE = √(C/(C − 1)·Σ_c (z_c − θ̂·n_c)²) / Σ_c n_c;
- null-imposed residuals r_c = z_c − θ0·n_c are multiplied by Rademacher signs ε_c, and t* is recomputed with the same studentisation;
- p = (1 + #{t* ≥ t})/(B + 1) with B sign vectors, or the exact proportion over all 2^C sign vectors when 2^C ≤ B (`confirmatory.wild_cluster_p`).

**Registered decision rule ("conservative", Operationalisation of protocol §5.3).** In the simulations the protocol's textbook BCa interval rejected true nulls above the nominal 0.025 with 25–40 clusters, and about twice as often with 25 families of strongly unequal size (Section 3.3 summary; README §6). The registered rule keeps the protocol's BCa condition and adds the others, so it can only be stricter than the protocol's literal rule:

- **H1:** supported iff the lower 95% limits of BCa, expanded BCa and cluster jackknife-t are all > 0 **and** the wild cluster bootstrap-t p ≤ 0.025. The wild bootstrap is added because the three interval methods together were still slightly above the nominal 0.025 in the scenarios with few or strongly unequal families (Section 3.3 summary), and the wild cluster restricted bootstrap-t is designed for that case. The reported interval is [min of the three lower limits, max of the three upper limits]; the reported p is the largest of the four p-values. The BCa interval is also reported.
- **H4:** the same rule without the wild bootstrap (its estimator is not a ratio of cluster sums); the interval and p are those of the intersection–union of BCa, expanded BCa and jackknife-t.
- H2's effect size and the H1 sensitivity analyses use the same rule.
- The simulated size and power of every rule are in `operating_characteristics.json`.

**Small-sample rule** (protocol §5.3: fewer than 20 clusters). If an analysis has C < 20 clusters, the bootstrap intervals are replaced by the cluster jackknife-t; for H1 the wild cluster bootstrap-t is still required (exact enumeration when 2^C ≤ B). If the bootstrap distribution is degenerate (all replicates equal), the jackknife-t is used.

**Clustered exact interval for a proportion** (used for H3 and DR3; `confirmatory.cp_design_effect`; the Korn & Graubard 1998 construction). For a proportion p̂ = k/n over C clusters with per-cluster counts (k_c, n_c):

1. Design effect: deff = max(1, V_clu/V_srs), where V_clu = C/(C − 1)·Σ_c (k_c − p̂·n_c)²/n² and V_srs = p̂(1 − p̂)/(n − 1). Set deff = 1 if C < 2 or p̂ ∈ {0, 1}.
2. Effective size: n_eff = (n/deff)·(t_{0.975, n−1}/t_{0.975, C−1})², with k_eff = p̂·n_eff.
3. Interval, with exact beta quantiles at real-valued parameters:
   - lower limit Beta⁻¹(0.025; k_eff, n_eff − k_eff + 1), or 0 if k_eff = 0;
   - upper limit Beta⁻¹(0.975; k_eff + 1, n_eff − k_eff), or 1 if k_eff = n_eff.
4. One-sided p for H0: rate ≤ θ0 is F_Beta(θ0; k_eff, n_eff − k_eff + 1), or 1 if k_eff = 0. So the lower limit exceeds θ0 exactly when p < 0.025.

With one response per cluster this reduces to the exact Clopper–Pearson interval (Clopper & Pearson 1934).

#### 5.1.2 H1 (primary)

- **Data.** A1, Vietnamese, short answer, greedy. All conflict atoms with a frozen decoy × all open models that were run: four planned, or three under DR6, with Llama replaceable under DR11. Decoy-less conflict atoms are excluded and counted.
- **Estimate.** Δ̂ = π̂_f − π̂_d, as defined in Section 4.3: response-level pooling, with the chance baseline D weighted by k_i (`confirmatory.h1_delta`).
- **Decision.** The registered rule of Section 5.1.1, clustered by conflict family (small-sample rule below 20 families).
- **Also reported:**
  - π̂_f, π̂_d (weighted and unweighted) and E with the same interval method;
  - per-model Δ̂_m and E_m (descriptive);
  - the share of label-4 responses by system and by source version;
  - the one-sided p-value.
- **Robustness (registered, `confirmatory.h1_robustness`).**
  - **S1, directional correction** (num/bp conflict atoms): ρ̂ = (n_f + 0.5)/(n_d + 0.5), where n_f and n_d count single-valued label-5 answers that are not decoy matches and fall on the foreign side or the decoy side of the MoH value; Δ_dir = π_f − ρ̂·π_d, with ρ̂ recomputed in every bootstrap resample.
  - **S2:** H1 on num/bp conflict atoms whose decoy came from the arithmetic mirror rule (`decoy_rule == "mirror_arith"`).
  - **S3:** H1 on conflict atoms with `roundness_ok` (the decoy is at least as "round" as the nearest conflicting foreign value, roundness = the largest step m·10^j, m ∈ {1, 2, 5}, dividing the value).
  - **N1:** H1 without atoms whose conflicting foreign value overlaps a neighbouring MoH value.
  - **"H1 robust"** is written only if S1 and S2 are both computed and both have a lower limit > 0 by the registered rule. S3 and N1 are reported next to them in the main results table.

H1 is a family of its own with no multiplicity adjustment. There is no gatekeeping between H1 and the secondary family.

#### 5.1.3 H2

- **Data.** A1, short answer, greedy. US-unique conflict atoms with a frozen decoy × open models. Pairs (atom, model) with valid Vietnamese and English responses.
- **Counts.** For family k:
  - n_k is the number of pairs;
  - b_k is the number of pairs with FUS = 1 in English and 0 in Vietnamese;
  - c_k is the number of pairs with the reverse (0 in English, 1 in Vietnamese).
- **Primary test: clustered McNemar** (Durkalski et al. 2003; `confirmatory.durkalski`, checked against the published examples).
  - With w_k = (b_k − c_k)/n_k: z = Σ_k w_k / √(Σ_k w_k²), with one-sided p = 1 − Φ(z). The Durkalski χ² is z².
- **Operationalisation: exact fallback and edge cases.**
  - If fewer than 20 families have any discordant pair, the p-value comes from an exact sign-flip randomisation test on the same w_k (`confirmatory.sign_flip_exact`). Under H0 each w_k is symmetric about 0, so p = #{ε ∈ {−1, +1}^K : Σ ε_k w_k ≥ Σ w_k} / 2^K, enumerated fully over the K families with w_k ≠ 0.
  - If there are no discordant pairs at all, p = 1.
- **Effect size.** P(FUS | EN) − P(FUS | VI), pooled over models, with a family-clustered interval by the registered rule.
- **Sensitivity analyses.** Pairs in which both answers have `parse_method = answer_line` and neither is truncated (B.19); a paired logistic GLMM, FUS ~ language + model + (1 | atom) + (1 | family).

#### 5.1.4 H3

- **Data.** A3, Vietnamese, short answer, greedy, conflict atoms, each open model m. No other input can widen this set.
- **Estimate.** r̂_m = the proportion of responses with label ∈ {3, 4}.
- **Per-model test.** H0_m: r_m ≤ 0.05 (`hypotheses.H3_margin`). The one-sided p_m and the 95% CI come from the clustered exact interval (Section 5.1.1), with conflict families as clusters.
- **Why not the bootstrap here (Operationalisation).** Percentile-type bootstrap intervals can under-cover for proportions near 5% with 25–40 clusters. The design-effect Clopper–Pearson interval is never narrower than exact Clopper–Pearson (deff ≥ 1) and adds a degrees-of-freedom correction for few clusters, so it is the conservative choice. This departs from the protocol's generic "BCa, or t with fewer than 20 clusters" rule for this one proportion test and for DR3.
- **Combining models: partial-conjunction p-value** (Benjamini & Heller 2008, Bonferroni form).
  - With M open models and u = ⌈M/2⌉: p_H3 = min(1, (M − u + 1)·p_(u)), where p_(u) is the u-th smallest p_m.
  - M = 4 gives p_H3 = 3·p_(2); M = 3 gives p_H3 = 2·p_(2).
- **Validity.** The Bonferroni partial-conjunction p-value is valid under any dependence between the per-model p-values (the models share atoms), provided each p_m is valid; Holm then needs only valid marginal p-values. The per-model p_m from the design-effect interval is an approximation; its size was checked by simulation at the least-favourable configuration (one model far above the margin, the others exactly at it), with equal and with unequal family sizes (README §6).
- **Why not the literal criterion.** The literal reading ("the CI lower bound is > 5% in at least half of the models") does not control the error rate for the "at least half" claim. It is reported descriptively, as the number of models whose 95% lower limit exceeds 0.05.
- **English.** A3 English responses are reported descriptively.

#### 5.1.5 H4

- **Data.** Served answers: A2, greedy, in the question's language (Vietnamese and English), × open models, restricted to answers that state a value (labels 1–5).
  - Atoms: all frozen atoms (conflict, concordant, no-counterpart) *except* `indistinguishable` ones, which protocol §1.2 bars from every confirmatory test.
  - Groups as in Section 4.3.
  - Self-abstentions (label 6) are excluded and reported by group as coverage loss.
- **Estimate.** RR_MH, the Mantel–Haenszel risk ratio of E (labels 3–5) with strata model × language (Section 4.3). Stratification removes confounding by between-model and between-language differences in disagreement share and base risk; the crude pooled RR is reported descriptively.
- **Test.** θ = log RR_MH and θ0 = log 2 (`hypotheses.H4_rr_lower_bound`). The one-sided p comes from the registered rule over a bootstrap with **guidelines** as clusters (**Operationalisation**: conflict families do not exist for non-conflict atoms, and guideline clusters also capture dependence between the two languages and the models). It is reported with the 95% interval of RR_MH.
- **Operationalisation: edge cases.**
  - If the agreement side of the full-sample Mantel–Haenszel ratio is 0, every estimate (full sample, resamples and jackknife) adds 0.5 to both groups' error counts and 1 to both groups' totals in every stratum.
  - If the disagreement group is empty, H4 cannot be estimated and p = 1.
- **Secondary losses and estimators (reported, not decisive):** loss W (labels 3–6 over all served answers) with the crude pooled RR — the estimator registered in the first draft, which counts self-abstentions as errors and routes them into the disagreement group by construction; loss W with the Mantel–Haenszel RR; Vietnamese served answers only (B.17).

#### 5.1.6 Multiplicity: Holm in the family {H2, H3, H4}

- **Operationalisation:** one-sided p-values, with a familywise α of 0.025 (one-sided), which matches the protocol's 95% two-sided convention.
- **Procedure.** Order p_(1) ≤ p_(2) ≤ p_(3) and reject H_(j) iff p_(i) ≤ 0.025/(3 − i + 1) for every i ≤ j (Holm 1979).
- **Adjusted p-values.** p̃_(j) = max_{i ≤ j} min(1, (3 − i + 1)·p_(i)). A hypothesis is confirmed iff p̃ ≤ 0.025 (`confirmatory.holm`).
- **Fixed family size.** The family always has three members. A hypothesis that cannot be computed enters with p = 1. If DR3 triggers, H4 is still tested inside the family but is interpreted as exploratory. The family is never shrunk after seeing data.

#### 5.1.7 RQ3: certified selective answering (secondary aim)

**Setting.**

- The served answer is A2 in the question's language, at temperature 0.
- The analysis is run **per open model** (**Operationalisation**). Each model is a separate deployment, and its simultaneous guarantee covers its own G·K = 2 × 4 = 8 bounds at confidence 1 − δ = 0.90. Across the four models the bounds are not simultaneous: the probability that at least one model's set of bounds fails can reach 1 − 0.9⁴ ≈ 0.34, and this is stated with the results.
- **Primary unit: one Vietnamese served answer per atom that states a value** (**Operationalisation**). One answer per atom keeps calibration items independent, which the exact binomial bound needs. The loss is E (labels 3–5). The model's self-abstention rate is reported next to the bounds. The same procedure on English answers is secondary.
- Pooled certification across models is not done, because the responses would be dependent.
- **Atoms:** all frozen atoms except `indistinguishable` ones, the same population as H4. A sensitivity analysis includes them.
- **Scope of the guarantee.** Regime (a) certifies the selective risk for a question drawn at random from the same model's frozen non-reference atom pool at the freeze date (a finite-population statement). It says nothing about other questions, other corpora or later guideline versions.

**Groups.** Agreement versus disagreement, rule-based and label-free (Section 4.3), fixed before calibration.

**Score c(x)** (protocol §4.6; **Operationalisation**; `confirmatory.rq3_scores`).

- **Model:** a logistic regression per model predicting C (concordant), fit **only on the reference split**, with the objective 0.5·‖w‖² + C·Σ log-loss (L2 penalty, C = 1.0, intercept not penalised), minimised by L-BFGS with at most 1,000 iterations (`confirmatory.fit_logistic_l2`). Features are standardised with reference-split means and standard deviations.
- **Features:** s1, s2 and s3; consistency across the 5 A2 samples; the mean token log-probability of the answer span, with missing values imputed by the reference-split median and a missingness indicator added.
- **Cross-fitting on held-out guidelines** (protocol §4.6). Within the reference split, scores used to set thresholds are out-of-fold, from a deterministic GroupKFold by guideline with min(5, number of reference guidelines) folds (`confirmatory.group_kfold`: guidelines sorted by size, each assigned to the fold with the fewest rows). The model refit on the whole reference split then scores the calibration and test atoms. The score therefore never uses calibration or test labels, as the guarantee requires.

**Thresholds** (`vnsoc.ltt.thresholds_from_reference`).

- For each group g and coverage c_k ∈ {1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25} (`rq3.coverages`): λ_gk is the (1 − c_k) quantile of the reference-split scores in group g, with λ = −∞ for c = 1.
- An item is answered iff c(x) ≥ λ.

**Primary RQ3 output: simultaneous Clopper–Pearson upper bounds** (`vnsoc.ltt.certified_bounds`, δ = 0.10).

- On the calibration split, for each (g, k), n_gk is the number answered and k_gk the number of those with E = 1:

  U_gk = Beta⁻¹(1 − δ/(G·K); k_gk + 1, n_gk − k_gk),

  with U = 1 when n_gk = 0 or k_gk = n_gk.
- Guarantee: Pr(for all g, k: R_g(c_k) ≤ U_gk) ≥ 1 − δ.
- Reported as, for example, "at 50% coverage, the disagreement group's risk of a non-MoH value is certified ≤ U". The table gives n, k, the empirical risk and U, plus the coverage achieved on the calibration and test splits.

**Secondary: Learn-then-Test thresholds** (Angelopoulos et al., arXiv 2110.01052; `vnsoc.ltt.ltt_thresholds`).

- **Grid.** Per group, from `make_grids`: the quantiles of reference-split scores at 60 equally spaced levels from 0.85 down to 0. The sequence goes from strict (answer the top 15%) to lenient.
- **Test at each grid point.** Exact binomial p = F_Bin(k; n, α_g) ≤ δ/G, tested in fixed sequence and stopping at the first failure. The last threshold that passes is λ̂_g, which is +∞ if none passes. δ = 0.10, Bonferroni over G = 2.
- **α per group.** α_g = 0.10 (`rq3.alpha`), unless group g has fewer than 300 calibration atoms for that model. In that case α_g = 0.15 (DR4). The rule depends only on counts, not labels (`rq3.alpha_fallback`, `rq3.alpha_fallback_min_cal_atoms`).
- **Usefulness (DR5).** The minimum useful coverage is 0.30 (`rq3.min_useful_coverage`). **Operationalisation:** certified coverage is the test-split coverage of λ̂ in the disagreement group, and 0 if λ̂ = +∞. Below 0.30, the conclusion is that the abstention layer is not useful for that group; this is still a result and is reported.
- **Also reported:** the largest c_k with U_gk ≤ α_g.

**Regime (a): random atom split.**

- **Registered split.** Violation is judged against the risk on the **whole pool** (calibration ∪ test), not against the test half. For each (g, k), a violation is R_pool > U_gk. All 8 indicators and the familywise indicator are reported.
- **Re-randomisations (implementation check, `confirmatory.rq3_rerandomise`).** The reference split, score model and thresholds are held fixed while the calibration and test halves of the non-reference atoms are redrawn 500 times (seeds in Section 2.6). The empirical familywise violation frequency against the pool risk is reported; the theoretical target is at most δ = 0.10. Because atoms are drawn without replacement from a fixed pool, this checks the implementation; it is not evidence that the guarantee transfers to questions asked at deployment.
- **Cluster-split sensitivity.** The whole regime-(a) analysis is repeated with the split made by cluster (conflict family, otherwise guideline), which protocol §4.7 prefers; the score model is refit on the new reference split.

**Regime (b): leave-guideline-out** (`confirmatory.rq3_regime_b`). This regime has no theoretical guarantee: cluster-level guarantees need far more than 25–35 guidelines (Dunn, Wasserman & Ramdas, arXiv 1809.07441).

- There are 500 guideline-level splits (`rq3.regime_b_splits`, Section 2.6). In each, the score model, including its GroupKFold reference scores, is **refit on the reference guidelines only**, so the test guidelines are unseen by the score. Thresholds and grids come from the reference guidelines, bounds and Learn-then-Test thresholds from the calibration guidelines, and violations are judged on the test (unseen) guidelines.
- Splits in which either group is absent from the reference or calibration guidelines are skipped. The number of splits used is reported.
- **Operationalisation: two violation frequencies**, each over the splits used:
  1. **Bound violation:** any of the 8 bounds U_gk is exceeded by the empirical risk on test guidelines at λ_gk.
  2. **Learn-then-Test violation:** in any group, the empirical test-guideline risk at λ̂_g exceeds α_g.
- **Acceptance.** Each frequency is compared with 0.20 = 2δ (`rq3.regime_b_max_violation_rate`). Transfer to unseen guidelines is declared acceptable for a layer only if its frequency is ≤ 0.20. Per-bound frequencies are also reported.

**Comparators** (descriptive).

1. No abstention.
2. "Abstain on every disagreement item": serve only the agreement group.
3. Single-group Learn-then-Test (G = 1, level δ), with its violation rate inside the disagreement group reported.
4. Conformal risk control on the joint loss E·1{answered} at level 0.10 (Angelopoulos et al., arXiv 2208.02814).
5. Specialty grouping in the style of HG-CRC. Groups are guideline specialty categories fixed from the catalogue before any output; the same bound and Learn-then-Test procedure is used with δ/(G_spec·K). Per-group selective risk control of this kind has recognised failure modes when groups are small (Gurram, arXiv 2608.14639), which is why the bounds at fixed coverage are the primary output.

**Cross-tabulation.** Signal group × conflict status × response type (labels 3, 4, 5, 6). It shows the known gap: a consistently stubborn model falls in the agreement group.

**DR3 applies to RQ3** (Section 5.1.9).

#### 5.1.8 Regression models (descriptive, R)

**Data.** Open models, short answer, greedy, conditions A1–A3, both languages, conflict atoms.

**Model** (protocol §5.3):

```
wrong ~ model * language + model * condition + slot_type
        + (1 + language | guideline) + (1 | atom),   family = binomial
```

**Outcome and coding.**

- wrong = W (labels 3–6); a second fit uses E on value-stating answers.
- Treatment coding with reference levels: model = qwen3_8b, language = vi, condition = A1, slot_type = the most frequent slot type among frozen conflict atoms (determined before any output).

**Estimation order.** Each step is used only if the previous one fails (**Operationalisation** of the convergence criteria).

1. `lme4::glmer` (Laplace approximation; Bates et al. 2015; `glmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa", optCtrl = list(maxfun = 2e5))`). It fails on an error, an lme4 convergence warning, or `isSingular()`.
2. `glmmTMB::glmmTMB` (Brooks et al. 2017) with the same formula. It fails on an error, a non-zero optimiser convergence code, or a Hessian that is not positive definite (`pdHess = FALSE`).
3. `glmer` without the random slope: (1 | guideline) + (1 | atom).
4. `glmmTMB` without the random slope.

The step used is logged in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

**Error types.** Three separate binary GLMMs with the same right-hand side, for label 3, label 4 and label 5, each versus all other responses. **Operationalisation:** the label-3 model is restricted to atoms that have a superseded value; otherwise the outcome is structurally 0.

**Robustness check.**

- A GLM with the same fixed effects, with cluster-robust covariance from `sandwich::vcovCL` (Zeileis, Köll & Graham 2020):
  - clustered by guideline with type "HC2", the CR2-type bias-reduced adjustment (Pustejovsky & Tipton 2018), available for glm objects;
  - two-way, guideline + atom, with type "HC1".
- If the GLMM and GLM disagree in the sign, or in whether the 95% interval of a model × language or model × condition contrast excludes zero, the disagreement is reported.
- `clubSandwich` is not used, because it does not support these mixed models.

**Reporting.** Odds ratios with 95% Wald intervals. Predicted probabilities at random effects = 0.

No confirmatory decision depends on the GLMMs.

**Controls and manipulation checks** (descriptive, per model).

- **Concordant atoms** (Vietnamese value equals the international value): the correct rate at A1 is a competence and negative control.
- **A3 on concordant atoms:** a check that the oracle passage works.
- **Decoys:** the chance-coincidence control.
- **Multiple-choice order reversal:** a position-bias check (agreement of the chosen role across the two orders).
- **Answer-line compliance and truncation:** format manipulation checks, per model × language.

### 5.2 Transformations

- **Grading normalisation** (Section 6.3):
  - mg/dL ↔ mmol/L, using the analyte in the atom's context;
  - per-kg doses ↔ absolute doses when the question gives a weight;
  - "½ ampoule" → mg using the concentration printed in the guideline;
  - drug names → International Nonproprietary Names (INN);
  - schedules → sequences of days.
- **Number formats.** Vietnamese ("1.000,5") and English ("1,000.5") number formats are both parsed.
- **Comparison signs.** Signs (<, ≤, ≥, >) are parsed and kept with the value. Values are compared as points or closed ranges, with one registered exception (`vnsoc.grade._open_touch`): a point value with a strict sign that touches a proper range at one of its ends from outside is disjoint from that range. So "< 130" is not inside "130 to < 140" (their gap is one unit of the last recorded decimal), whereas point-against-point thresholds such as "> 2000" and "≥ 2000" stay equal. Conditional clauses of the MoH text are encoded in the value set at the context check (Section 3.1 item 2).
- **Labels** are recoded to the binary indicators in Section 4.3.
- **Categorical variables** use treatment coding with the reference levels in Section 5.1.8.
- **Other transformations.**
  - RR is analysed on the log scale.
  - RQ3 score features are standardised with reference-split statistics.
  - Months-to-cutoff is continuous and centred at 0 (issue date = cutoff).
- **Outliers.** No other transformation or outlier treatment is applied; there are no outliers in binary outcomes.

### 5.3 Inference criteria

- **H1.** The registered intersection–union rule of Section 5.1.1 (BCa, expanded BCa and jackknife-t lower limits > 0 and wild cluster bootstrap-t p ≤ 0.025), equivalent in intent to the protocol's "lower limit of the 95% CI > 0" at one-sided α = 0.025. H1 is a family of its own.
- **Substantive estimands.** Because H1 is expected to be supported at a pilot-scale effect, the results are interpreted through π_f and the excess over chance E with their 95% intervals. **Smallest effect of practical interest (Operationalisation; `hypotheses.H1_sesoi_excess`):** E = 0.10, that is, foreign matches exceeding chance by 10% of the non-decoy responses. E is described as practically important if its lower limit is ≥ 0.10, as not important if its upper limit is < 0.10, and as inconclusive otherwise. This interpretation does not change the H1 decision.
- **Robustness wording.** "H1 robust" is used only as defined in Section 5.1.2. If H1 is supported with family clusters but not with guideline clusters (B.10), the paper states: "The excess of foreign matches over chance is supported when conflict families are the resampling unit, but not when guidelines are; the evidence therefore depends on treating families as independent."
- **H2–H4.** One-sided p-values (Section 5.1). Holm step-down at a familywise one-sided α of 0.025 across {H2, H3, H4}. A hypothesis is confirmed iff its Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.025.
  - H3's p-value is itself a partial-conjunction p-value over models.
  - Unadjusted 95% CIs are reported alongside.
- **Descriptive estimates.** 95% intervals without multiplicity adjustment, labelled descriptive.
  - Per-model, API-model, multiple-choice and GLMM estimates.
  - Small counts use exact Clopper–Pearson intervals.
- **RQ3.**
  - Primary: simultaneous 1 − δ = 0.90 coverage of the 8 bounds per model.
  - Learn-then-Test: familywise level δ = 0.10 per model.
  - Regime (b) acceptance: ≤ 0.20.
- **All tests are one-sided,** in the direction stated by each hypothesis.
- **Negative results.** Results against a hypothesis are reported with the same prominence. A precise negative result (narrow interval near 0) is a finding in itself.

### 5.4 Data inclusion and exclusion

**Atom level.**

- **Kept:** atoms that are span-verified, pass the context check, come from a guideline in force at the freeze, and belong to the frozen atom file. Atoms that fail verification are fixed or removed *before* the freeze, without any outputs being seen.
- **H1–H3** use only `conflict_status == "conflict"`. Concordant and no-counterpart atoms are excluded from H1–H3 and reported descriptively. They stay in H4, RQ3 and the controls.
- **Conflict atoms without a decoy** are excluded from H1 and H2 (their decoy count is 0 by construction) and counted in the freeze addendum. They stay in H3, H4 and RQ3.
- **Indistinguishable atoms** are excluded from every confirmatory test, H1–H4 (protocol §1.2), and from the primary RQ3 analysis. They are reported descriptively and included in an RQ3 sensitivity analysis.
- **H2** additionally requires the US-unique criterion.
- **DR7:** atoms affected by a later guideline stay in, and a sensitivity analysis excludes them.
- **Atom errors found after the freeze.** The frozen atom set stays the primary analysis set. Every atom found to be wrong after the freeze (its value, population, status or counterpart) is listed with the reason and the date found, and an analysis with the corrected atoms is reported as a sensitivity analysis. No correction is applied silently.

**Question level.** Questions that fail the leak, population, required-term, translation or ambiguity checks are removed before the freeze and listed in the QC table. An English question removed this way removes that atom's H2 pairs.

**Response level.**

- Every valid response is kept; there is no outlier removal.
- Partial overlaps and multiple values without an attributed MoH value are graded by the frozen rules. They count as not concordant (label 5, or 3/4 for a single partially overlapping value that matches another source), and their counts are reported separately.
- Answers that state a value while hedging ("not sure, maybe…") are graded on the value, as coded.
- Unit mismatches in the answer line get label 5, as coded.
- Responses flagged `needs_llm` are graded after LLM extraction (Section 6.3); DR9 applies. Extractions that remain empty become label 6 and are counted; a sensitivity analysis removes them (B.24).
- Responses truncated at 128 tokens are graded as they are; truncation is reported per model × language, and H2 is repeated without truncated pairs (B.19).

**Cell level.** A model × condition × language × format cell below 98% valid records after one rerun of its failed requests stays in the analysis with its valid records. It is flagged, its missing share is reported, and the missing-data bounds of Section 5.5 are applied to every confirmatory analysis that uses it, whatever the 2% trigger.

**Model level.**

- A model that cannot be run is replaced (DR10, DR11) or dropped (DR6). The pooled set is the models actually run, and this is reported.
- A model whose `needs_llm` rate exceeds 5% triggers DR9. The model is not excluded.

### 5.5 Missing data

- **Retries.** A request that returns an API or generation error is retried once. If it fails again, the response is missing.
- **Reporting.** Missingness is reported per model × condition × language × format cell. The target is at least 98% valid per cell.
- **Primary analyses** use all valid responses and assume missingness is unrelated to correctness; there is no imputation.
- **Pairs.** H2 and other paired analyses drop pairs with a missing member.
- **Bounds on missingness — Operationalisation** (`confirmatory.missing_bounds`). If an analysis cell has more than 2% missing responses (or is flagged under Section 5.4), the analysis is recomputed under two extreme imputations and both results are reported:
  - **H1** (A1, Vietnamese, open models, conflict atoms with a decoy): all missing set to foreign matches (raises Δ), then all set to decoy matches (lowers Δ);
  - **H3** (A3, Vietnamese, per model): all missing set to label 4 (raises r_m), then to label 2 (lowers r_m);
  - **H4** (A2 served answers with a known signal group): missing answers in the disagreement group set to label 4 and in the agreement group to label 2 (raises RR), then the reverse (lowers RR).
- **Missing signals.** For RQ3, if a signal input is missing (for example the English A2 answer), the signal is computed from the available inputs and the missingness is reported. For example, s2 does not fire when the English answer is missing.
- **Missing clinician annotations.** Analyses that need them are not performed if no clinician is available (DR12); the code surrogates are reported instead, labelled as surrogates.

### 6.1 Reference standard

- **What counts as the standard.** MoH diagnosis and treatment guidance (decisions and circulars) in force on 15 October 2026 for the stated population. Validity comes from each document's own "replaces" clauses and is recorded per atom, including partial amendments.
- **Two current documents disagree.** Every current value counts as correct (DR8), within the same level of care. When one value has to be displayed, priority goes to (1) the topic-specific document for that population, then (2) the most recent document.
- **Neighbouring contexts.** MoH values for another step, population or level of care are not part of the atom's value set; they are recorded so that an answer that coincides with them can be separated from a foreign match (Section 5.6 B.21).
- **MoH values that may lag current evidence.** These are flagged by a clinician (`moh_lags_evidence`), or, without a clinician, approximated by `moh_older_than_counterpart`; results are reported with and without them.
- **Measurement goal.** The study measures fidelity to Vietnamese guidance, not medical correctness.

### 6.2 Conflict definition and tolerance

Both are computed only by code, in `vnsoc.grade`, and are never set by hand. `vnsoc.match.decoys.finalize` recomputes them after the decoy is set. Values flagged `derived` are ignored throughout.

**Gap between two value items (`_gap`).**

| Value kind | Gap |
| --- | --- |
| `num` | Interval distance max(0, max(lo) − min(hi)), after converting both items to the atom's unit using its context (weight, concentration, analyte); ∞ if they cannot be converted. Exception (Section 5.2): a point with a strict sign touching a proper range from outside has a gap of one unit of the last recorded decimal |
| `bp` | max(\|Δsystolic\|, \|Δdiastolic\|) |
| `schedule` | 0 if the day sequences are identical, else ∞ |
| `drugs` | 0 if the key-drug sets are identical, else ∞ (a regimen differing by any drug lies outside the other) |
| `cat` | 0 if the labels are equal, else ∞ |

**Tolerance (`compute_tolerance`; `grading.yaml` tolerance_rule).**

- For `num` and `bp` atoms: half the smallest positive finite gap between any MoH item and any other item (foreign, superseded or decoy), so no tolerance window can overlap the MoH set.
- For the other kinds, or when no positive finite gap exists: 0.
- A response value matches an item if it lies inside the item's range widened by the tolerance on each side (open bounds). For `bp`, each component must lie within the tolerance.

**Conflict status (`conflict_status`).** Checked in this order:

1. No foreign value → `no_counterpart`.
2. No foreign item has gap > 0 to every MoH item → `concordant`.
3. Some conflicting foreign item has gap = 0 or gap < 2·tolerance to any superseded or decoy item → `indistinguishable`.
4. The decoy touches the MoH set (gap 0) → `indistinguishable`. A superseded value touching the MoH set does not change the status.
5. Otherwise → `conflict`.

A conflicting foreign value within 2·tolerance (gap 0 for other kinds) of a neighbouring MoH value flags the atom (`moh_neighbour_overlap`) but does not change its status in the primary analysis (Section 5.6 B.21).

### 6.3 Grading rules (grader_version 1.0.0; `configs/grading.yaml`, `vnsoc.grade`)

**Rules as coded.**

1. **Text preparation.** `<think>…</think>` blocks are removed.
2. **Answer line.** The answer span is the *last* line matching `ĐÁP ÁN`, `Đáp án`, `DAP AN`, `ANSWER` or `Final answer` followed by a colon (ASCII or full-width). Otherwise the whole output is the fallback span.
3. **Parsing** by value kind:
   - `num`: numbers in either Vietnamese or English format, ranges, comparison signs and units. A number without a unit is read in the atom's unit and flagged `unit_assumed`. Values in convertible units are converted; values in non-convertible units are dropped.
   - `bp`: systolic/diastolic pairs.
   - `schedule`: day sequences of at least 2 elements, unless the atom sets another minimum.
   - `drugs`: INN names via the synonym and combination tables in `grading.yaml`.
   - `cat`: the atom's category options.
4. **Several candidates without an answer line.** If there is no answer line and more than one distinct value, the response is flagged `needs_llm`.
   - It is sent to a fixed LLM extraction prompt (question and output only; no reference value), run on a low-cost API in batch mode.
   - It is then regraded with the extracted string (`parse_method = llm`), exactly as coded.
   - An empty extraction therefore gets label 6. The number of unresolved extractions is reported.
   - The extractor is checked by hand on 500 responses (Section 3.1).
5. **No value found.**
   - If the answer line holds a number in an unconvertible unit → label 5 (`unit_mismatch`).
   - Else, if the response asks which country or guideline applies *and* the condition is A0 → label 1.
   - Else → label 6.
6. **Several distinct values, not all in the MoH set** (`multi`). Each sentence of the answer span is cut at every named source; each piece is attributed to the name that opens it (a Vietnam/MoH marker, a named foreign body or country, or none).
   - Label 1 if at least one value is in the MoH set and attributed to a Vietnam/MoH marker or to no name, and every non-MoH value is attributed to a named foreign body or country. So "According to the MoH: 5–10 or 15 ml/kg/h" is not context-aware.
   - Otherwise label 5, reported separately.
7. **Drug lists containing both regimens.** A single drug list containing both the MoH regimen and a conflicting foreign regimen gets label 1 only if the attribution condition of rule 6 holds (the foreign regimen sits in a piece opened by a named foreign body); otherwise label 5.
8. **A single value, or all values in the MoH set:**
   - **In the MoH set → label 1 or 2.** It gets label 1 ("aware") if some sentence of the response names a foreign body or country **and** either states a non-MoH value with an explicit unit or contains an explicit contrast phrase ("khác với", "khác so với", "không giống", "trong khi", "còn theo", "ngược lại", "whereas", "unlike", "in contrast", "differs from", "different from", "as opposed to"). Otherwise it gets label 2.
   - Named foreign bodies are matched case-sensitively as acronyms with letter boundaries (WHO, ADA, AHA, ACC, ESC, ESH, CDC, AASLD, EASL, APASL, RCUK, NICE, ACOG, GINA, GOLD, WAO, EAACI, IDSA, USPSTF, KDIGO, US, USA, UK) or as names ("Mỹ", "Hoa Kỳ", "châu Âu", "Anh quốc", "Vương quốc Anh", "Tổ chức Y tế Thế giới", "American", "European", "British", "United States", "World Health Organization", "quốc tế" and "international" except in "đơn vị quốc tế" and "international unit(s)"). Words such as "who", "us", "while", "however" or "khác" alone never count.
   - Else, matching a superseded value → label 3.
   - Else, matching a foreign value → label 4, recording every matching system and source version.
   - Else → label 5, flagged `decoy_match` if it matches the decoy.
9. **Partial overlap** with the MoH set is flagged `partial` and counts as not concordant.

**Multiple choice.** The letter is read from the answer line, or from the first 40 characters if there is none. It is graded by the option's role:

| Role of chosen option | Label |
| --- | --- |
| MoH | 2 |
| Superseded | 3 |
| Foreign | 4, with its systems |
| Decoy | 5, flagged `decoy_match` |
| Filler | 5 |
| No valid letter | 6 |

**Version control.** The exact regular expressions are those in `vnsoc/grade.py` as frozen at the question freeze, with the SHA-256 in the freeze addendum (and the pre-freeze version in Section 5.0). Code corrections before the freeze are made without access to main-study outputs. Any rule change after the freeze increments `grader_version` and triggers full regrading (DR9).

### 6.4 Decoy construction rule (as coded in `src/vnsoc/match/decoys.py`, hashed in Section 5.0)

The decoy is chosen by `choose_decoy(atom)`, before any output exists. Derived values never anchor a decoy.

1. **Choose the pair to mirror.** The conflicting foreign items are those with gap > 0 to every MoH item. If there are none, no decoy is made (the atom is not a conflict atom). Among all pairs of a conflicting foreign item f and a MoH item v, choose the pair with the smallest gap(v, f); ties go to the first pair in record order.
2. **Rule order.** `choose_decoy` tries, in this order, (i) "auto", (ii) "mirror_geom", (iii) "mirror_far", and keeps the first candidate that passes `check_decoy` (step 5). The rule that produced the decoy is frozen with the atom (`decoy_rule`).
3. **Blood-pressure atoms.** "auto" = "mirror_arith": decoy = (2·v_sys − f_sys, 2·v_dia − f_dia). "mirror_far": (3·v_sys − 2·f_sys, 3·v_dia − 2·f_dia), twice the foreign distance on the other side. ("mirror_geom" does not apply to blood pressure.)
4. **Numeric atoms.**
   1. Convert v and f to the atom's unit using its context (for example, 0.01 mg/kg × weight). If conversion is impossible, no decoy is made.
   2. Let c_v = (v.lo + v.hi)/2, c_f = (f.lo + f.hi)/2 and w = f.hi − f.lo, and let nd be the largest number of decimal places among the four converted endpoints.
   3. "auto": c = 2·c_v − c_f ("mirror_arith"); if c − w/2 ≤ 0, it becomes "mirror_geom".
   4. "mirror_geom": c = c_v²/c_f, provided c_f > 0 (otherwise no decoy); nd = max(nd, 1) when c < 10.
   5. "mirror_far": c = 3·c_v − 2·c_f, twice the foreign distance on the other side.
   6. The decoy is [round(c − w/2, nd), round(c + w/2, nd)] in the atom's unit, with no comparison sign. If c − w/2 ≤ 0 or the rounded lower end is ≤ 0, that rule gives no decoy.

   Example (test fixture): MoH 15 ml/kg/h against foreign 5–10 ml/kg/h gives the decoy 20–25 ml/kg/h.
   "mirror_far" is not "about as far" as the foreign value (protocol §1.2); decoys from it are therefore outside sensitivity S2, and their number is reported.
5. **`check_decoy`** rejects a candidate that has gap 0 to the MoH set; has gap 0 to any recorded foreign or superseded value (including older foreign versions); has gap 0, or a gap < 2·tolerance for num/bp, to an MoH value of a neighbouring context; or makes the atom `indistinguishable` when the atom was not already indistinguishable without it.
6. **Schedule, drug and category atoms.** A person or agent proposes a real-looking alternative that belongs to no source recorded for the atom, before outputs exist, and `check_decoy` applies (`decoy_rule = "agent_proposed"`).
7. **No acceptable decoy.** The atom keeps its conflict status, is excluded from H1 and H2, and is counted (Section 5.4).
8. **Plausibility and regeneration (HG3.5).** Every conflict-atom decoy is rated against the rubric of Section 3.1 item 3, blind to outputs. An implausible decoy is regenerated by `vnsoc.match.atom_flags.regenerate_decoy`: (i) for num/bp, round it to the granularity of the source values (the smallest roundness of the MoH items and the nearest conflicting foreign item) — accepted if `check_decoy` passes (`decoy_rule = "rounded"`); (ii) otherwise, borrow a foreign value of another frozen atom with the same value kind, slot type and unit (protocol §4.2), choosing the candidate whose distance from the MoH set is closest to that of the nearest conflicting foreign value (ties: atom identifier, record order) — accepted if `check_decoy` passes (`decoy_rule = "borrowed:<atom_id>"`). The new decoy is rated again. A decoy still rated implausible stays, with `decoy_plausible = false`, and is excluded by the mandatory sensitivity analysis B.11.
9. **Finalise.** `finalize(atom)` recomputes tolerance and conflict status after the decoy is set. The frozen decoy is never changed after outputs are seen.

**Known limitation.** A rule-based decoy can be less attractive than the foreign value (on the other side of the MoH value, less round, or farther away), which would lower π_d. Sensitivity analyses S1–S3, B.11 and the multiple-choice replication address this.

### 6.5 Multiple-choice rules (as coded in `src/vnsoc/qgen/mcq.py`, hashed in Section 5.0)

**Eligibility.** Multiple-choice questions are built for conflict atoms and atoms with a superseded value, in Vietnamese and English, in two orders. An atom without a decoy gets no multiple-choice question.

**The four options.** All must be pairwise distinct (gap > 0); otherwise the atom gets no multiple-choice question and is listed in the QC table.

1. **MoH:** the first MoH value item.
2. **Foreign:** the nearest distinct conflicting foreign value (derived values excluded). Items identical across systems are merged, with the role written `foreign:SYS1+SYS2`; items are ordered by their smallest gap to the MoH items.
3. **Third option:** the first candidate that is distinct from the options already chosen. Candidates are the superseded values in record order, then the remaining conflicting foreign values in order of nearness.
4. **Decoy:** the first decoy item.

**Filler when only three options exist.** A filler is inserted before the decoy.

- For `num` and `bp` atoms it follows a rule: reflect the MoH value through the foreign value (c = 2·c_foreign − c_MoH in the atom's unit, with the foreign value's width, rounded to 4 decimals). If that is not positive, reflect through the decoy instead. For `bp`, both components must be positive.
- For other kinds a filler must be supplied and pass the distinctness check.

**Orders** (Section 2.6).

- Order 0 is the seeded permutation (seed 20260926); order 1 is its reverse.
- Letters A–D. Option text is rendered from the value item in the question's language, and the role of each letter is recorded.

**Prompt.** The stem, the options, and "Chỉ chọn một phương án. Dòng cuối: ĐÁP ÁN: <chữ cái>" (Vietnamese) or "Choose one option only. Last line: ANSWER: <letter>" (English). At A1 the stem is prefixed with the A1 cue.

**Grading.** As in Section 6.3.

### 6.9 Ethics

- **Participants.** There are no human participants and no patient data; only public documents are used. An ethics exemption is requested from the university.
- **Intended use.** Every artefact is labelled "for research and evaluation, not for clinical decision-making".
- **Scope of claims.** Matching the MoH does not imply medical correctness. MoH items that lag evidence are reported separately.
- **Terminology.** Answers that differ from MoH guidance are called "non-concordant with MoH guidance", never "incorrect", "wrong" or "harmful", in every output, table, figure and teaching material.
- **Limitations stated in advance.** A1 is a controlled probe and is not how students usually ask; A0 and A6 are closer to practice and are reported as descriptive or exploratory. Without a clinician, the harm and evidence-lag variables are code surrogates.

**Where AI assistants are used.**

- Claude Code agents (Anthropic) are used for:
  - writing and testing code;
  - drafting documents, including this registration;
  - literature scouting;
  - AI-agent review panels at milestones.
- Low-cost LLM APIs are used for atom extraction, counterpart proposals, question paraphrasing, translation and answer extraction.

**Safeguards.** Every AI-produced value is verified:

- by code: verbatim span matching, unit and number checks, and citation lookup against Crossref, DataCite or arXiv; or
